title: "The path: engineer to Wharton"
---

Josh is a chemical engineer (P.Eng) who spent six years in Canadian energy: three building the Trans Mountain Expansion pipeline with Kiewit, then three in new energy technologies and AI adoption at Enbridge, before betting on himself with a full-time MBA at the Wharton School (Class of 2028), where he is majoring in AI for Business and Finance.

The application story he tells straight: no banking or consulting pedigree, a GMAT that started at a 485 diagnostic and took three rounds and hundreds of after-work hours to drag to a 655. He got into Wharton with over $200,000 in scholarship offers across the schools he applied to, by building the entire strategy around his non-traditional background instead of hiding it. Alongside work he chaired Young Professionals in Energy Calgary's flagship dinner and was elected the chapter's 2026 President, and he co-founded Interstitial Energy, a hydrogen-storage startup that went through the Avatar Innovations venture studio. He now coaches MBA applicants on the same playbook.

How he operates, from the principles he keeps in his vault: focus beats novelty ("eliminate shiny object syndrome"); speak less, listen more (his hardest one, see his blind spots); skills compound, so build slowly and deliberately; treat every role as a calling, not a job. His faith is the foundation under all of it, something he writes about openly in his published essays.

Where he is now: first year at Wharton, living in Philadelphia since late July 2026. The vehicle he chose for the next step is management consulting with an energy focus, picked deliberately rather than by default. He treats it as tuition for the durable skills: judgment, client trust, and getting change to actually land inside an organization.

Where he's pointed after that: the intersection of energy and AI, specifically deployment work where his technical depth is the moat.
