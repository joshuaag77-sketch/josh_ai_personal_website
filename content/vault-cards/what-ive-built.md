Josh's rule is that AI has to ship into a real workflow or it is a demo. What is running:

A one-tap capture pipeline. Share any YouTube video from his phone and thirty seconds later a structured, AI-formatted note lands in his knowledge vault, title, summary, key ideas, tags, source. Built with an Android automation tool, a transcript API, and Claude. It took five hours of debugging and has run ever since. He wrote the build up publicly.

A gas turbine sparing simulator. A decision-support web app for a real compressor fleet: eight field engines backed by two or three spares, where running out means a station goes offline. It optimizes a 10-year maintenance schedule against real constraints (overhaul intervals, shop turnaround times, utilization), then runs 500 Monte Carlo simulations to quantify the probability of a zero-spare event, informing a multi-million-dollar engine purchase decision. Public on his GitHub.

The second brain itself. An Obsidian vault kept alive by seven scheduled agents with named roles. A nightly gardener promotes raw daily notes into permanent knowledge and repairs links. A Sunday brief finds connections between notes he never linked, checks his career theses against the week's news, and lands in his inbox. A case room transcribes his recorded consulting practice cases locally, scores them, and banks reusable moves. A lecture worker turns his handwritten class notes and slides into one compounding note per course. A calendar curator filters roughly a hundred school events a week down to the ones worth attending. A study-room keeper keeps his campus room allowance booked in the best free slot. A health check watches all of it and pings his phone only when something breaks. On top of the agents, a weekly close-the-loop review he runs by hand forces a decision (promote, act, or kill) on everything they surfaced. The graph is explorable in 3D on this site at /brain.

This website. Including the chatbot, the curated public interface to the vault.

The pattern across all of it: capture once, structure it, connect it, let it compound.
